# THE SIGNAL FILES — Critical Review and Next Visual Pass

## Overall assessment

The redesign has established a credible visual language: warm paper against an ink-dark field, Archivo Black headlines, cyan/red/amber evidence semantics, physical-print comparisons, dossier motifs, and restrained collage treatments. It is no longer a generic dark dashboard.

The remaining issue is structural rather than stylistic. Many screens still use a familiar application formula—header, progress bar, title card, data card, answer grid, fixed footer—then decorate those rectangles with tape, stamps, rotation, and grain. The next pass should make each act feel staged as an investigative scene, not merely themed as one.

## 1. What still feels like a website or educational dashboard

- **Persistent application chrome:** the thin masthead, segmented progress bar, act counter, theme control, and fixed bottom action rail frame every scene like a web application. They are useful, but visually too continuous and prominent.
- **Repeated card grammar:** decision screens consistently read as “question card + evidence card + four answer buttons.” The paper treatment changes the surface, not the underlying quiz/dashboard rhythm.
- **Desktop symmetry:** the recurring left-content/right-widget split is clean but conventional. It makes evidence feel supplemental rather than the dramatic object driving the scene.
- **Report screen:** horizontal skill bars, numeric values, two summary boxes, and a completion label remain the clearest dashboard moment in the experience.
- **Generic evidence states:** acts using “ΤΡΕΧΟΝΤΑ ΣΤΟΙΧΕΙΑ” or “ΦΑΚΕΛΟΣ ΕΥΡΗΜΑΤΩΝ” resemble reusable information widgets more than case-specific discoveries.
- **Fixed action footer:** the full-width footer is operationally strong but visually resembles a product toolbar, especially on desktop.

## 2. What succeeds and should be preserved

- **Typography and palette:** Archivo Black gives the project identity, while IBM Plex Mono supports labels and evidence metadata. Cyan for verified evidence, red for alerts/false claims, and amber for uncertainty now feel intentional and understandable.
- **Cold Open:** the torn viral post, red unverified stamp, live repost count, and cinematic imbalance produce urgency without resorting to cyberpunk or intelligence-agency clichés.
- **Evidence Reveal:** the paired `VIRAL POST` and `ARCHIVE MATCH · 98%` prints are the strongest translation of analysis into a physical investigative action.
- **Checkpoint:** the three pinned sheets clearly separate known, suspected, and unknown information. This is both pedagogically precise and visually native to the case-file concept.
- **Feedback semantics:** strong/caution stamps and the distinct principle block communicate judgment without reducing the result to right/wrong.
- **Cliffhanger concept:** the intercepted-post burst and CASE 02 reveal successfully shift from closure to a new signal.
- **Restrained motion:** short paper/stamp entrances support tactility without becoming flashy, with reduced-motion handling already present.

## 3. Hierarchy and density, especially at 390×844

- The mobile decision screen gives **question sheet, evidence strip, four choices, hint, masthead, and footer** nearly equal visual weight. The eye has no single dominant focal point after reading the title.
- The cyan borders, paper fills, black outlines, stamps, clips, shadows, and rotated edges compete when several appear together. The result is legible but visually noisy.
- Evidence diagrams are compressed to preserve one-screen behavior. At mobile size, Timeline labels become micro-annotations and the Network loses its legend and explanatory caption; both read faster as decoration than evidence.
- Feedback is well grouped, but four stacked rows inside one bordered sheet recreate an instructional rubric. The result stamp is prominent while the actual evidentiary distinction is comparatively quiet.
- The fixed footer creates a strong horizontal cutoff and visually detaches the command from the scene above it.
- On desktop, large unused dark areas coexist with relatively small evidence objects. The one-screen constraint is met, but the scene does not fully occupy its stage.

## 4. Character treatment

The characters are visually consistent, but they currently function more as speaker badges than narrative presences. All portraits come from one group image, are cropped into similarly sized cutouts, and sit inside the same dialogue component; this creates cohesion but not character specificity.

For the next pass, each character should gain a repeatable visual signature without changing dialogue: a distinct crop scale/silhouette, one restrained annotation motif, and a consistent placement role. Unit Lead can anchor briefing sheets, MARA can appear attached to source prints, LEO can occupy diagram margins, and NOOR can appear beside response language. Larger appearances should be reserved for narrative turns so the portraits feel authored rather than routinely stamped onto UI.

## 5. Season Hub

The Hub currently sits between a case wall and a marketing page. The collage hero, large brand lockup, value-proposition sentence, CTA cluster, “active cases” section, and team band follow landing-page sequencing. The active dossier and sealed cases are on-theme, but they arrive after a conventional hero rather than forming the main world of the game.

The next pass should make the case wall itself the first composition: CASE 01 as the dominant open dossier, future cases partially visible as sealed folders, current progress physically attached to the active file, and the team material integrated as pinned case contributors rather than a separate promotional band. Preserve all existing copy, but redistribute it into the board.

## 6. Network and Timeline as signature gameplay screens

They are improved, but not yet signature screens.

- **Timeline:** the red spike and event sequence are immediately readable, but the central visual is still a compact bar chart inside a bordered card. Its labels are very small on mobile, and the causal relationship between the influencer repost and the spike is conveyed mainly by text rather than composition.
- **Network:** the radial map clearly distinguishes a broadcast cascade from a dense cluster, but it resembles a static infographic widget. Equal-size peripheral nodes, a centered hub, and a conventional legend make it analytically tidy but not narratively specific.
- **Needed shift:** each should become the scene background or primary evidence surface, occupying roughly half the viewport. Choices should feel physically pinned to the diagram’s conclusion zone. Timeline should use a marked evidence trail from post → high-reach repost → spike → later foreign amplification. Network should use uneven clusters, directional marks, one visibly dominant broadcaster, and sparse handwritten callouts placed at the exact visual evidence they interpret.
- On mobile, these should use purpose-built simplified compositions, not merely scaled-down desktop charts with hidden legends.

## 7. Palette, typography, and decorative system

The palette is purposeful and should remain. The three accents have clear jobs and the warm-paper/ink contrast avoids both corporate blue and cyberpunk neon.

The type pairing is also strong, but the mono face is overused at very small sizes, reducing readability and making some screens feel like data UI. Reserve mono for timestamps, source IDs, and compact metadata; use the main sans for interpretive labels.

Tape, stamps, rotation, grain, grid lines, clipped corners, heavy outlines, and offset shadows are individually appropriate. Used together on nearly every object, they become a repeated visual effect rather than evidence of handling. Decoration should indicate function: tape attaches, stamps classify, marker highlights a conclusion, rotation distinguishes loose evidence, and grain unifies the world.

## 8–9. Concrete next-pass proposal

### High-impact structural changes

1. **Turn the Hub into the case wall itself.** Remove the separate hero/feature-section rhythm; let the open CASE 01 dossier, sealed future files, brand title, team references, and primary action coexist in one asymmetric board composition.
2. **Create three act composition families instead of one repeated template.** Use briefing scenes, evidence-analysis scenes, and decision/verdict scenes with distinct spatial rules while retaining the same controls and act order.
3. **Promote Timeline and Network to full scene surfaces.** Give each a bespoke desktop and mobile composition, larger marks, fewer tiny labels, direct annotation of the key inference, and choices visually connected to the evidence.
4. **Reframe feedback as a verdict overlay on the evidence.** Keep every feedback sentence, but place the stamp and principle as the primary layer, with supporting evidence/cannot-conclude notes attached around it rather than four equal rows.
5. **Make characters scene participants.** Use larger, character-specific cutouts only at narrative beats, attach their commentary to the evidence they own, and vary placement by role instead of repeating one dialogue card.

### Secondary polish

6. **Reduce decorative frequency by roughly one third.** Limit each scene to one dominant physical metaphor and one secondary mark; remove redundant tape, rotation, stamp, and shadow combinations.
7. **Soften the application chrome.** Keep progress, theme, keyboard behavior, and fixed actions, but integrate progress into the dossier edge and make the action rail feel like the bottom tab of the current sheet rather than a separate toolbar.
8. **Clarify type roles and mobile scale.** Increase critical evidence labels, reduce tiny mono text, and use fewer simultaneous uppercase labels. Preserve one-screen behavior through composition and selective simplification, not by shrinking explanatory evidence.

## 10. Remove or simplify

- Simplify the separate Hub team band; retain its copy and portraits, but integrate them into the case-wall composition.
- Remove decorative tape from objects that are already framed, clipped, stamped, or shadowed.
- Reduce low-opacity grid backgrounds where they compete with diagrams or small text.
- Remove duplicate classification labels when the same status appears in the act ruler, stamp, and card heading.
- Simplify sealed future dossiers so one strong seal and partial file label carry the idea; repeated hatch, rotation, opacity, lock icon, and seal together are excessive.
- Reduce report chrome: keep all scores and feedback, but replace the dashboard-like boxed summary layout with one completed dossier and a clear evidence-profile page.
- Avoid hiding explanatory legends on mobile. Replace them with fewer, larger, directly labeled marks.

## Guardrails and validation for implementation

- Preserve all Greek copy, game logic, decisions, correctness, scoring, state fields, localStorage keys, keyboard controls, accessibility, navigation, and act order.
- Keep every act within 390×844 without page scrolling or fixed-action overlap, then verify at 1068×639 and 1280×720.
- Check decision, feedback, expanded `ΓΙΑΤΙ;`, Timeline, Network, Report, and Cliffhanger states in both themes and with reduced motion.

## Proposed visual direction

The next pass should feel like opening and manipulating one evolving investigation file, not moving through a themed web interface. Use fewer decorative signals, larger case-specific evidence, and stronger scene-to-scene composition so the narrative—not the card system—becomes the memorable visual identity.
